Display of B

Depth scan (B)

Shifts B as if the segment lay this many voxels (2.4 µm) deeper into the sheet (+) or out in front of it (−). A stays on the true surface; the colouring stays that of the true surface. A new depth takes ~1 min the first time (cached after); "compute all" fills every depth in the background so the slider and play are instant.

Pre-filter

Removes both fiber families from every depth plane and the height map before the method runs (first use per segment and width: ~6 s).

Flattens small dense specks (mineral grains) to their surroundings on every depth plane: bright features shorter than the size in every direction. Fibers, strokes and larger patches stay. Runs before the notch (first use per segment and size: ~10 s).

Settings are per method and remembered. Percentiles refer to this region. A saved variant appears under ★ Starred.

Every filter is a fixed formula: none is trained on ink labels. Each first finds the papyrus face under every pixel and reads fixed depths from it. Lengths are physical (µm), so a filter looks at the same scale at any resolution. recommended marks the three filters that held up best across scrolls.

Add a segment

A surface volume: the CT layers sampled along a traced segment. Its layers are stored next to the examples and prepared once (a minute or two for a 2 cm square).

Select every layer at once (e.g. 00.tif … 64.tif). They are stacked in file-name order.

A folder of layer images, a .tif or .npy stack, or a Zarr (OME-Zarr pyramids: give the level).

Layer spacing is the voxel size of the scan for the usual layers/ folder. A Zarr with metadata fills both by itself. Layers far from 2.4 µm are resampled to 2.4 µm planes, the depth the filters are written for.
